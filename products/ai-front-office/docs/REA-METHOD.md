# REA method and evidence boundary

This product uses REA's discipline, not proprietary Avoca code. No Avoca binary, private API, credential, source code, or customer data was supplied or inspected.

## Observation

- Public product/provider documentation, public pricing, market research, official statistics, and the behavior of this implementation.
- Local test evidence for triage, booking races, read-back verification, webhooks, admin security, notifications, spam, and dropped-call recovery.

## Derivation

- A booking state is derived from independent evidence: system write/read-back, recording, transcript, complete intake, fee acceptance, and customer confirmation queue.
- Addressable-shop counts are derived only where the research labels the arithmetic and source units.

## Inference

- “Proof, not autonomy” should convert and retain tiny shops better than an AI-employee promise.
- Confirm-first onboarding should earn trust before direct booking.
- Jobber distribution and vertical templates should beat generic paid acquisition for this ICP.

## Unknown

- Real call latency, voice quality, interruption behavior, transfer reliability, carrier deliverability, consent compliance, and Jobber mutation behavior remain unknown until tested with live accounts.

## Competitive reconstruction rule

Reconstruct the customer outcome and failure contract, not a competitor's protected expression. Every inferred behavior gets either a deterministic test, an explicit unknown, or a safe fallback. Product improvement comes from closing observed trust gaps: visible proof, bounded authority, owned failures, transparent pricing, owner-controlled accounts, and a narrow vertical setup path.
